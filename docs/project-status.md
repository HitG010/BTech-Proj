them in full.

## 1. The research question and how the code serves it

The proposal asks whether a small frozen language model can learn *when* extended
reasoning pays off, and whether the training evidence for that decision can be
collected more cheaply than by sampling every question the same number of times.
The candidate contribution is the acquisition rule, not the router itself.

Everything built so far is the measurement instrument that question needs:

- generate a **direct** answer and a **thinking** answer for a question, with exact
  token and time costs;
- score both against a separate gold file without the parser ever seeing gold;
- do that for hundreds of questions, resumably, under a token and time budget, with
  a run identity that makes a re-run of the same protocol land in the same place;
- turn the results into the numbers the research design needs: the thinking-versus-
  direct gap, the per-question oracle, how many questions the decision matters on,
  how well a confidence signal already routes, statistical power, and cost.

Milestones A–C of the plan (offline pipeline, real inference, resumable pilot) are
done and verified. Milestones D–F (baselines and controller, acquisition, transfer
and confirmation) are not started; the pilot results reshape them, see section 7.

## 3. Architecture

About 2,200 lines of source and 600 of tests in `src/reasoning_budget/`. Data flows
config → questions → jobs → generation records → scores → reports.

### `config.py` — strict experiment configuration
Dataclasses for the experiment, model, decoding, and limits. Unknown keys are
rejected, every numeric field is validated, and the resolved configuration becomes
part of the run identity. Protocol v2 added `closure_text` (validated to contain
`</think>`) and `limits.max_consecutive_failures`.

### `data.py` — immutable datasets
Ten arithmetic fixtures for smoke tests, and GSM8K frozen into four disjoint splits
from one seed: 100 pilot, 400 controller-training and 100 validation questions from
the official training split, 500 from the official test split. Questions are
normalised (Unicode, case, whitespace) and de-duplicated across splits before
sampling, preferring the official test questions. Gold answers live in a separate
file that generation never opens. The split manifest is tracked in version control;
the questions, gold and runs stay local.

### `backend.py` — two-stage generation
The Transformers backend runs one question at a time. For thinking mode it runs a
reasoning stage that stops at the single `</think>` token, an EOS id, the deadline,
or the reasoning allowance; at the allowance it inserts the configured closure text
(v2: the Qwen3 report's budget phrase) and runs a separate answer stage. Direct mode
uses the same chat template with thinking disabled, which prefills an empty think
block, so "direct" means Qwen3 non-thinking mode with whatever short working the
model chooses. Every EOS id declared by the checkpoint ends a stage. For direct
answers two confidence values are stored: the mean token log-probability after the
sampling warpers, and the same quantity from the raw logits, captured one step at a
time by a logits processor so memory stays flat. A `GENERATION_PROTOCOL` constant is
part of every run identity. A fake backend exercises the whole pipeline offline.

### `runner.py` — resumable, budgeted runs
Jobs are one (question, mode, sample) each, with a seed derived from the experiment
seed and the question content so job order and interruptions do not change them.
Records are written atomically with SHA-256 envelopes; a corrupt or incomplete file
is regenerated on resume, a valid one is skipped. Every job reserves its maximum
token allowance before it runs; the ledger of attempts is scanned once per invocation
and then tallied in memory. An isolated failed job is recorded and the run continues;
a streak of failures (default three) stops the run. Run identity is the resolved
config, backend metadata (chat template hash, decoder configs, closure and EOS ids),
question list, environment, and protocol version; the source hash is recorded for
provenance but no longer keys the run.

### `scoring.py` — gold-free exact numeric scoring, version `numeric-v4`
Boxed answers and labelled claims ("final answer:", "####") must agree; the value is
compared to gold as an exact rational. Currency and percent signs, spacing macros and
`\text{}` wrappers inside a claim are ignored, a single number with a plain unit word
is accepted, prose after an answer label is not a claim. Two different values, a
second number, an equals sign, or a truncated response stay unscorable. Every parser
change bumps the version and the audit binds to it.

### `reporting.py` and `analysis.py` — from records to evidence
`rb report` scores a run, averages repeated samples within questions, bootstraps
whole questions for intervals, computes the paired thinking-minus-direct difference,
checks the human audit file, and writes Markdown, JSON, CSV and a figure.
`rb analyze` adds the per-question outcome matrix, oracle best-mode accuracy, the
decision-relevant fraction, the forced-closure rate against the 5% gate, token and
time distributions, direct-confidence AUROC, a power estimate for 500 test questions,
a programme cost projection at observed and scenario throughput, and a pre-screen
that flags the rows a human auditor should read first.

### `cli.py` — commands
`doctor`, `prepare`, `smoke`, `generate`, `score`, `report`, `audit`, `analyze`.

### Tests
89 offline tests, no network or weights: parser cases including the two defects
found this week, a scripted tiny model driving the real stopping hooks and both
stages, forced and natural closure, EOS handling, the raw log-probability recorder,
interruption and resume, corrupt files, budget reservations and the in-memory tally,
the failure-streak policy, identity rules, split determinism and de-duplication,
whole-question bootstrap, and the pre-screen helpers. Ruff check and format pass.

| Run | Config | Jobs | Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|

| `af1f1e56c5d453bbfd5e` | pilot v1, 100 GSM8K × 2 × 2 | 400/400 | direct 63.0%, thinking 46.0%: a protocol artifact, see below |
| `2116b22fe18c9b25a354` | smoke v2 | 20/20 | direct 9/10, thinking 10/10, no truncation, exposed one scorer defect |
| `f73a5665230cd83a7f12` | pilot v2, same 100 questions | 400/400 | direct 83.5%, thinking 93.0%, paired +0.10 [0.03, 0.16] |

Three offline fake-backend runs and one interrupted calibration run also exist.

### What pilot v1 taught
The v1 prompt asked for "only the numerical answer" and allowed 256 answer tokens.
The model ignored the instruction in both modes; direct wrote a full solution and
thinking restated its solution after `</think>`. A third of direct answers and half of
thinking answers were cut off, so thinking looked 17 points worse than direct, and
forced closure at 2,048 reasoning tokens hit 15%. Ninety of the 105 cut thinking
answers had already stated the right answer inside the reasoning. The whole
research question depends on the sign of this gap, so the protocol was changed and
the pilot re-run.

### What pilot v2 shows (details in `docs/pilot-v2-report.md`)

| Mode | Accuracy | Truncated at 1,024 | Forced closure at 4,096 | Mean tokens | Mean seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Direct | 83.5% | 2 / 200 | 0 | 301 | 12.9 |
| Thinking | 93.0% | 1 / 200 | 14 / 200 (7%) | 1,751 | 75.6 |

- Thinking is better on 18 of 100 questions, direct on 5, equal on 77. The oracle
  that picks the better mode per question reaches 96.0%.
- The direct answer's mean log-probability predicts its correctness with AUROC 0.83.
  Routing on it, with a threshold chosen on the same questions, reaches 93.0% while
  sending 40% of questions to thinking, at about 1,140 tokens per question versus
  1,751 for always thinking.
- The minimum detectable accuracy difference on the 500-question test set is about
  four points; the gap between the confidence baseline and the oracle is three.
- Thinking costs 5.8× the tokens and time of direct on this model.
- Forced closure is concentrated on five questions the model treats as contradictory;
  seven of the fourteen still scored correct after the budget phrase.
- An agent pre-screen of 82 responses (all 47 wrong ones, all 14 flagged, 30 random
  correct) found no scorer defect; details in `docs/pilot-v2-prescreen.md`.
- Full-programme cost at observed lengths: about 13 million tokens per model, 157
  local hours for the 1.7B and an assumed 394 for the 4B, versus 1.8 and 4.6 GPU-hours
  at a 2,000 tokens/s scenario, about $6.4 at $1/hour.

## 5. Defects found and fixed this week

| Defect | Effect | Fix and commit |
|---|---|---|
| 256-token answer allowance and "only the number" prompt | truncated a third of direct and half of thinking answers | protocol v2 configs, `1e10b9d` |
| Reasoning allowance 2,048 | 15% forced closure | 4,096 in v2; 7% now, decision pending |
| Scorer rejected `\boxed{\$18}`, `18\%`, `\text{}` | wrong "invalid" verdicts | numeric-v3, `21bea0c` |
| Scorer read "the answer is correct, so…" as a claim | false "conflicting" verdict | numeric-v4, `ed6f2a6` |
| Budget ledger re-read every attempt file per job | quadratic cost, hours on large runs | in-memory tally, `84680cf` |
| One failed job halted the run | idle cloud rental on a single timeout | failure-streak policy, `84680cf` |
| Run identity hashed source files | any edit orphaned every run | protocol version + backend metadata, `84680cf` |
| Single EOS id passed to generation | second declared EOS ignored | EOS list from checkpoint, `84680cf` |
| Confidence only after sampling warpers | inflated, renormalised values | raw-logit recorder, `84680cf` |
| Split manifest ignored by version control | frozen IDs unversioned | tracked manifest, `1e10b9d` |

Rescoring pilot v1 under v3 and v4 flipped no correctness value, so the v1 numbers
in section 4 are comparable.

## 7. What comes next

The proposal's week-2 deliverable, a verified pilot report and a full-run cost
estimate, exists after two days. The pilot changed the plan in three ways, and the
next steps follow from them.

### 7.1 Decisions before more code (this week)
- Choose the task mix. GSM8K alone leaves at most three points for a learned
  controller over a free confidence signal. The Qwen3 report gives this model a
  20-point thinking gap on MATH-500. The recommended path is GSM8K plus one
  wider-gap numeric set (the numeric-answer subset of MATH, or GSM-Plus), or the
  procedural tasks promoted from confirmation to primary.
- Choose the primary metric. Tokens at matched accuracy, or the area under the
  accuracy–cost curve, is well powered and is where a controller that decides from
  the question alone has a structural advantage; report accuracy alongside.
- Keep 4,096 reasoning tokens and report the forced-closure rate, or test 8,192 on a
  cloud GPU where the tail is cheap.

### 7.2 Compute: the vLLM backend (next engineering item, needs a GPU)
`src/reasoning_budget/backends/vllm.py` with the same two-stage protocol using prefix
caching, batched over questions, engine name and version in the run identity, the
same sampling parameters, and a 20-output cross-check against the Transformers
backend on the same GPU. The runner needs a batched job loop with per-batch
reservations. Expected outcome: the whole 1.7B programme in a few GPU-hours.

### 7.3 Data: breadth pool and the second task set
Freeze `artifacts/data/gsm8k-v2` with a `v2-extend` split that keeps every v1 id and
adds about 1,100 further training candidates, so acquisition strategies can choose
between new questions and repeats. Freeze the second task set with the same
machinery: numeric answers only, de-duplicated against every existing split, gold
kept separate, manifest tracked.

### 7.4 Milestone D: baselines and the first controller (proposal week 3)
- Policies over cached traces: always direct, always thinking, random at several
  fractions, confidence-threshold routing charged for the direct answer it needs.
- Features: frozen MiniLM sentence embeddings plus question length, numeric-token
  and operator counts; ridge predictors for each mode's accuracy and cost with alpha
  chosen on the validation split only; the decision rule from the proposal with a
  sweep over the trade-off weight to draw the accuracy–cost curve.
- New modules `features.py`, `controller.py`, `policies.py`, `evaluate.py`; commands
  `rb fit` and `rb evaluate`; paired whole-question bootstrap for every comparison.
- Fold the confidence-routing and reasoning-claim computations from the one-off
  script into `rb analyze`.

### 7.5 Milestone E: acquisition (proposal week 4), gated
- Generate the replay cache once: up to eight pairs per training question on the
  cloud backend, recording every trace's cost.
- **Ceiling check first:** train the controller on one-sample labels and on
  eight-sample mean labels. If denoised labels do not improve it, label noise is not
  the bottleneck and the acquisition method cannot help in this setting; stop and
  report that.
- Then uniform, random and the proposed regret-per-cost acquisition, replayed from
  the cache under equal token ceilings, with simulated acquisition cost kept
  separate from the physical cost of building the cache.

### 7.6 Milestone F: transfer, confirmation, manuscript (proposal weeks 5–8)
Freeze the method before opening test results; SVAMP and procedural evaluation;
Qwen3-4B with its own controller; ablations removing uncertainty weighting, cost
normalisation and repeated observations; the DARS-style uniform-repeat baseline;
manuscript with the limitations the pilot already exposed (GSM8K gold artefacts,
forced closure on contradictory questions, two Qwen checkpoints not being a family
transfer claim).

### 7.7 Risks
- **Headroom.** If the second task set also shows a small controller-versus-confidence
  gap, the accuracy claim is out of reach and the paper's claim must be about cost.
- **Label noise is not the bottleneck.** The ceiling check may end the acquisition
  contribution; the plan says to report that honestly rather than replace it with a
  robustness study.
- **Compute access.** Without a GPU the cache cannot be built; the Mac can only run
  pilots.
- **Dataset artefacts.** A few GSM8K items have gold answers that depend on a reading
  the model rarely takes; they should be reported, not edited.
